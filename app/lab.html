Phone tests

Five tests that decide which voice and which transcription engine the Reader uses on this phone. Run them on Wi-Fi: tests 2 and 3 download small models (roughly 40 to 120 MB each, once; the page shows the real size as it downloads). Run one test at a time. When you are done, tap Copy all results and paste them to Claude.

2Neural voice speed (Piper)

Kokoro is already measured on this phone: too slow on the processor, and noise on the graphics chip. Piper voices are much lighter. Each test renders three sentences on the processor and times it. Faster than 2× real time means it can read live.

The sampler downloads each voice once (about 60 MB) and times it too.

3Transcription speed

The graphics chip crashed the phone here, so these run on the processor with small models. Record yourself talking for about 15 seconds (stop whenever you like), then try each model. The audio is saved first, as the app will always do.

4Listening with the screen locked

Plays soft beeps in 15-second pieces (piece 3 has three beeps, and so on) through the lock-screen player. Start it, lock the phone, wait about three minutes, unlock and come back. The log shows whether the pieces kept changing while locked. Then try the built-in voice the same way.

Long-file test: one 4-minute file with a beep every 15 seconds. Start it, lock the phone for about three minutes, then come back. The page compares how far the audio got with how long you were away.

5Recording in the background

Starts recording, saving a piece every 5 seconds. Switch to another app or lock the phone for about a minute, then come back and stop. A gap in the log means Android paused the microphone.
